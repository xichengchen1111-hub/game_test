# Period character import

`import_period_character.py` preserves a supplied Tripo ZIP and imports **three real source actions** as `01_Idle`, `02_Walk`, and `03_Talk`. It does not produce a walk or conversation by adding motion to Idle, and it does not retarget a clip from a different skeleton.

Supply `--idle-action`, `--walk-action`, and `--talk-action` with exact Blender-imported names when the automatic name match is missing or ambiguous. Names can gain an armature prefix during FBX import; an error lists the actual choices. `--idle-range START END`, `--walk-range START END`, and `--talk-range START END` select inclusive cycle endpoints in the original source frame rate. Each selection must be distinct and contain real bone motion. Walk must articulate both legs. These numerical checks do not establish that a hand gesture is semantically a conversation; review the actual selected clips.

The importer samples evaluated world-space bones, including movement on an armature or ancestor, then removes each clip's first-to-last **horizontal** Hips displacement. It keeps vertical hip motion, leg and foot articulation, facial bones, rest axes, mesh vertices and weights. It does not close a mismatched cycle with an arbitrary pose blend. Choose correct cycle endpoints and inspect the reported seam. Animated shape-key tracks currently require explicit matching support and are rejected rather than silently discarded.

`Character.blend` contains the packed editable source. `Character.fbx` contains all three baked actions. `Motion.json` beside the FBX supplies `walkMetresPerSecond` and `calibrationRequired` for runtime Walk playback. The runtime speed is scaled source horizontal travel divided by the **baked output cycle duration**, matching the actual clip consumed by C#. `sourceWalkMetresPerSecond` separately records travel divided by the original source cycle duration. Travel below 5 cm is recorded but treated as an in-place/seam residual: both speeds are `0`, calibration is required, and the game must not invent a natural gait speed. Source and baked durations are both recorded; resampling rounds to the nearest 30 fps frame.

`authoring-results.json` records the source file hash, exact action names/ranges, articulation, horizontal travel, loop pose/velocity discontinuities, and per-foot shoe-surface measurements at every 30 fps frame. A sole near the floor is only a candidate contact; it is not proof against sliding or self-intersection. An in-place walk cannot be tested for world-space sliding until its intended travel speed is calibrated. Bad floor/seam measurements are visible review failures, not repaired by changing skinning.

The default preview writes 30 JPEGs: five phases from the front and side for every action. These help inspect pose and silhouette but do not replace complete motion and transition playback, both-side/back views, clothing/hand clearance or Unity verification. `--skip-renders` is diagnostic only. All acceptance/runtime/self-intersection flags stay false.

For isolated experiments, use `--root` under `builds/` and `--fixture-label`. Existing local Meshy files have real walking curves and can exercise export mechanics; their look-around clips used as test channels are **not** proof of a new character's speaking animation. The accepted Tripo station clerk has real Idle/Talk but lacks Walk and must fail the new requirement. Never write these fixtures into the production PoliceCast folders or mark them accepted.

## Tripo image-to-model exports and arm clearance (2026-10-13)

Tripo's image-to-model FBX export names its atlas pages `tripo_image_<id>_0_0` (base colour) and `tripo_image_<id>_0_3` (tangent normal) instead of `*_BaseColor` / `*_Normal_Bake`; the importer accepts both. A text-to-motion clip keeps its prompt as the action name, so pass it with `--talk-action` (the saved "Calm Conversation Gestures" clip arrives as `Armature|A calm adult speaking to a person standing in front of`).

Tripo retargets motions to a bare standard body. On these coated characters the forearms sat 4–10 cm inside the coat skirt in every action and the hands passed through the coat front in Talk. `prepare_period_character.py` therefore runs `arm_clearance` on the three baked actions: per frame the upper arm turns about the shoulder, away from the body axis, by just enough that bare hands and fingers stay 4 mm outside the cloth and a sleeve presses at most 2 cm into it; the corrections are widened by four frames, Gaussian-smoothed and wrapped across the loop seam, then any residual is solved per frame. Mesh, weights and all other bones are untouched. Clearing the sleeves completely was tried first and rejected: the coat skirt is wider than a hanging arm, so it held the arms spread on every frame. `authoring-results.json` records the largest correction and residual per clip under `armClearance`.

`check_self_intersection.py` measures the result independently on the prepared `Character.blend`: every frame of the three actions, hand and forearm depth behind the nearest torso/hip/leg face, reported per part with close-up renders, and `withinLimits` when hands are at most 3 mm and sleeves at most 2.5 cm deep. It covers arms against the body only. Skirts, shoulders, collars and the overall stance still need the rendered sheets (the review now includes a back view) and an in-game look. The stout characters end up standing with their arms a little away from the body, most visibly on the last frames of Talk; narrowing the coat skirt in the mesh would be the way to bring them in further.
